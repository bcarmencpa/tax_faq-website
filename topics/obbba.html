OBBBA Tax Changes

New tax law provisions including SALT cap, retirement, and business changes

8 topics

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) is major federal tax legislation signed into law in 2025. It makes permanent, extends, or modifies many provisions from the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) that were set to expire after 2025, while also introducing new changes.

  • Most individual provisions are effective for tax year 2025 and beyond
  • Some provisions are retroactive or phased in over multiple years
  • Key areas affected: standard deduction, SALT cap, retirement accounts, business deductions, estate taxes, and energy credits
Tax law is complex and details continue to be clarified by IRS guidance. Contact our office to discuss how specific provisions apply to your situation.

The State and Local Tax (SALT) deduction cap — $10,000 under the TCJA — has been increased under the OBBBA.

  • The new cap is $40,000 for most filers (phasing down for very high incomes)
  • This primarily benefits taxpayers in high-tax states like New York, New Jersey, California, and Illinois
  • The higher cap makes itemizing more attractive for many taxpayers who previously couldn't exceed the standard deduction
  • The cap still applies to the combined total of state/local income taxes (or sales taxes) + property taxes
If you live in New York or NYC, this change is significant. We'll analyze whether itemizing now makes sense for your return — call our office.

The OBBBA builds on SECURE 2.0 (2022) with additional retirement-related provisions:

  • Enhanced catch-up contributions: Ages 60–63 can contribute up to $11,250 extra to 401(k)s (vs. the standard $7,500 catch-up for those 50+)
  • Auto-enrollment expansion: New employer plans must auto-enroll employees at 3%–10% deferral rates
  • Emergency savings accounts: Employers can offer linked emergency savings accounts — withdrawals penalty-free for emergencies
  • RMD age: Remains at 73, with further increases to 75 scheduled for those born after 1960 under SECURE 2.0
  • Roth employer matches: Employers can now offer Roth (after-tax) matching contributions

Key business tax changes under the OBBBA:

  • QBI deduction (Section 199A) made permanent: The 20% pass-through deduction is no longer set to expire — major benefit for self-employed individuals and small business owners
  • Bonus depreciation restored to 100%: Full first-year expensing of qualified property (it had been phasing down from the TCJA's 100% to 60% by 2024)
  • R&D expensing restored: Businesses can again immediately deduct domestic R&D costs rather than amortizing over 5 years
  • Business interest deduction: EBITDA-based limitation restored (more favorable than EBIT-based)
  • Section 179 limit increased: Expanded immediate expensing for smaller business equipment purchases

The OBBBA permanently extends the higher standard deduction amounts from the TCJA (which were set to revert to pre-2018 levels after 2025).

  • The elevated standard deductions remain in place and continue to be indexed for inflation annually
  • For 2025: $15,000 (single), $30,000 (MFJ), $22,500 (HOH)
  • Without the OBBBA, the standard deduction would have been approximately halved starting in 2026

However, with the SALT cap increasing to $40,000, more taxpayers may now find it worthwhile to itemize — particularly in high-tax states.

The OBBBA permanently extends the elevated estate and gift tax exemptions from the TCJA.

  • The combined lifetime exemption is $13.99 million per person for 2025 (would have dropped to ~$7 million in 2026 without legislation)
  • Married couples can shelter up to ~$27.98 million combined using portability
  • The annual gift tax exclusion is $19,000 per recipient in 2025 (indexed for inflation)
  • Estate tax rate remains at 40% on amounts above the exemption
The permanent higher exemption reduces urgency for aggressive gifting strategies, but lifetime giving still makes sense for estate planning in many situations. Consult our office.

The OBBBA made significant changes to the energy credits established by the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA 2022):

  • Electric vehicle (EV) credits: The $7,500 new EV credit and $4,000 used EV credit are being phased out or repealed for most vehicles — timelines vary by vehicle type
  • Residential clean energy credit (solar, battery storage): Modified — confirm current eligibility before making purchases
  • Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit: Rules tightened; some categories of upgrades may no longer qualify
  • Clean electricity production credits: Reduced or eliminated for new projects
If you were planning an EV purchase or home energy upgrade partly for tax reasons, contact us before spending — the credit landscape has changed significantly.

Action items to consider in light of the OBBBA:

  • Review your withholding: The new SALT cap and permanent QBI deduction may change your tax liability — update your W-4 accordingly
  • Reconsider itemizing: If you're in a high-tax state, the $40,000 SALT cap may now make itemizing worthwhile — run the numbers with us
  • Accelerate business purchases: With 100% bonus depreciation restored, 2025 and 2026 are ideal years to purchase qualified business equipment
  • Review energy-related plans: EV and home energy credits are changing — act quickly on purchases where you still qualify
  • Estate planning revisit: The permanent higher exemption changes the calculus — review your plan with our office
  • Maximize retirement contributions: Take advantage of enhanced catch-up limits if you're 60–63
